Proving Asymptotic Bounds

Overview

The definitions of \(O\), \(\Omega\), and \(\Theta\) are not just vocabulary. They are proof tools. To prove an upper bound, find constants that make \(f(n)\leq c g(n)\) eventually true. To prove a lower bound, find constants that make \(c g(n)\leq f(n)\) eventually true. To prove a tight bound, prove both.

This page collects the proof patterns from the LaTeX chapter and presents them in a form that is easier to reuse when analyzing algorithms.

Proofs from Definitions

Direct proofs usually work by simplifying \(f(n)\) until it is clearly bounded by a constant multiple of the comparison function. For polynomials, the basic idea is that lower powers are eventually at most the highest power. If \(a\leq b\), then \(n^a\leq n^b\) whenever \(n\geq 1\).

Example: Prove \(5n^2-3n+20=O(n^2)\)

For \(n\geq 1\), \[ 5n^2-3n+20 \leq 5n^2+20 \leq 5n^2+20n^2=25n^2. \] Therefore \(5n^2-3n+20=O(n^2)\), using \(c=25\) and \(n_0=1\).

The negative term was removed because it can only make the expression smaller. The constant \(20\) was replaced by \(20n^2\) because \(1\leq n^2\) for \(n\geq 1\).

Quick check: Why is it invalid to replace \(-12n\) with \(-12n^2\) when proving an upper bound for \(4n^2-12n+10\)?

For \(n\geq 1\), \(-12n^2\leq -12n\), so replacing \(-12n\) by \(-12n^2\) makes the expression smaller. That is the wrong direction for an upper-bound proof. It is safe to replace \(-12n\) with \(0\), because \(-12n\leq 0\).

Tight Bounds

For a tight bound, prove an upper bound and a lower bound. The upper bound often removes negative lower-order terms. The lower bound often removes positive lower-order terms and then shows that the dominant term eventually outweighs any negative terms.

Example: A Polynomial Tight Bound

Let \[ f(n)=n^8+7n^7-10n^5-2n^4+3n^2-17. \] We will show \(f(n)=\Theta(n^8)\).

For the upper bound, when \(n\geq 1\), \[ f(n)\leq n^8+7n^7+3n^2 \leq n^8+7n^8+3n^8=11n^8. \] So \(f(n)=O(n^8)\).

For the lower bound, keep the dominant term and bound the negative lower-order terms by a multiple of \(n^7\): \[ f(n)\geq n^8-10n^7-2n^7-17n^7=n^8-29n^7. \] If \(n\geq 58\), then \(29n^7\leq \frac{1}{2}n^8\), so \[ f(n)\geq \frac{1}{2}n^8. \] Therefore \(f(n)=\Omega(n^8)\), and together the two bounds give \(f(n)=\Theta(n^8)\).

The constants do not need to be best possible. A proof with \(c=1000\) and \(n_0=500\) is just as valid as a proof with smaller constants if the inequality is true.

Useful Properties

Several properties make asymptotic proofs shorter. They should be used after you understand the definitions, not as magic rules.

Property Statement How to read it
Transitivity If \(f=O(g)\) and \(g=O(h)\), then \(f=O(h)\). An upper bound of an upper bound is still an upper bound.
Scaling If \(f=O(g)\) and \(k>0\), then \(kf=O(g)\). Constant multipliers do not change growth rate.
Sums If \(f_1=O(g_1)\) and \(f_2=O(g_2)\), then \(f_1+f_2=O(\max\{g_1,g_2\})\). The faster-growing term controls the sum.
Products If \(f_1=O(g_1)\) and \(f_2=O(g_2)\), then \(f_1f_2=O(g_1g_2)\). Product bounds multiply.
Duality \(f=O(g)\) if and only if \(g=\Omega(f)\). Upper bounds and lower bounds are the same comparison read in opposite directions.

Proofs Using Limits

When the relevant limit exists, limits can make asymptotic proofs much shorter. Compute \[ \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f(n)}{g(n)}. \] Then interpret the result:

Limit value Conclusion
\(0\) \(f(n)=o(g(n))\), so \(f(n)=O(g(n))\) but not \(\Theta(g(n))\)
Finite positive constant \(f(n)=\Theta(g(n))\)
\(\infty\) \(f(n)=\omega(g(n))\), so \(f(n)=\Omega(g(n))\) but not \(\Theta(g(n))\)
Example: Limits Beat Algebra

Find a tight bound for \(x^4-23x^3+12x^2+15x-21\).

Compare it with \(x^4\): \[ \begin{aligned} \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{x^4-23x^3+12x^2+15x-21}{x^4} &= \lim_{x\to\infty}\left(1-\frac{23}{x}+\frac{12}{x^2}+\frac{15}{x^3}-\frac{21}{x^4}\right)\\ &=1. \end{aligned} \] Since the limit is a positive finite constant, the function is \(\Theta(x^4)\).

Limits are not a substitute for understanding. If the limit does not exist, or if you apply a rule such as l'Hopital's Rule outside its conditions, the result may be invalid. But for many functions that occur in algorithm analysis, the limit method is the most efficient proof.

Proof-Writing Habits

  • State the target: Say whether you are proving \(O\), \(\Omega\), \(\Theta\), or \(o\).
  • Use the right direction: Upper-bound proofs need \(f(n)\leq c g(n)\); lower-bound proofs need \(c g(n)\leq f(n)\).
  • Do not erase terms by hand-waving: You can ignore lower-order terms after proving the bound, but the proof itself needs valid inequalities or a valid limit.
  • Keep only input-size variables in final bounds: Temporary variables such as \(i\), \(j\), and \(k\) should not appear in the final asymptotic answer.
  • Use simple constants: Constants do not need to be optimal. Choose values that make the proof easy.

Reading Comprehension Questions

  1. Why is it not enough to say "lower-order terms become irrelevant" when writing a formal Big-O proof?
  2. When proving \(f(n)=\Theta(g(n))\) from the definitions, what two separate inequalities do you need?
  3. If \(\lim_{n\to\infty} f(n)/g(n)=0\), what asymptotic relationship follows?
  4. Why can the constants \(c\) and \(n_0\) be larger than necessary?
  5. What mistake is made when a final running-time bound contains a loop variable such as \(i\)?
  1. Answer: It is an intuition, not a proof. A formal proof must provide valid inequalities or a valid limit that justifies the bound.
  2. Answer: You need \(f(n)\leq c_2g(n)\) for an upper bound and \(c_1g(n)\leq f(n)\) for a lower bound, both eventually.
  3. Answer: \(f(n)=o(g(n))\), which also implies \(f(n)=O(g(n))\).
  4. Answer: The definitions only require some positive constants that work. They do not require the best possible constants.
  5. Answer: Loop variables are temporary implementation variables, not input-size variables. The final bound should be expressed in terms of input sizes such as \(n\), \(m\), \(|V|\), or \(|E|\).

In-Class Activities

  1. Repair a proof: A student says \(4n^2-12n+10\leq 2n^2\) for \(n\geq 1\). Find the invalid step and write a correct Big-O proof.
  2. Two methods: Prove \(7n^3+4n^2-8n+27=O(n^3)\) once from the definition and once using limits.
  3. Choose constants: For a proof of \(5n^2-3n+20=O(n^2)\), decide whether \(c=50\), \(c=2\), \(n_0=100\), and \(n_0=0\) can work. Justify each answer.

Homework Problems

  1. Prove that \(n\log n=o(n^2)\).
  2. Prove that \(\log(x^2+x)=\Theta(\log x)\).
  3. Prove that \(\sqrt{5x^2+11x}=\Theta(x)\).
  4. Prove that \(2^x=O(3^x)\) using both a limit and the definition of Big-O.
  5. Prove the transitive property for Big-O: if \(f=O(g)\) and \(g=O(h)\), then \(f=O(h)\).
